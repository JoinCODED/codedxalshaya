The five planted faults

Every fault in the Quarterly Property Operations Report is deliberate. This page tells you what they are, where they hide, and what each one teaches. Trainees find them; you confirm them.

Do not hand this out

Give trainees the PDF only. If they see this page first, the exercise is over — the whole point is that they discover the errors by checking, not by being told. Reveal it in the debrief.

The faults in full

F-01Arithmetic §3 Occupancy and income · §1 Executive summary

The rent roll total is wrong

Report says
Portfolio total annualised rent roll KWD 4,720,000. The executive summary repeats it as “KWD 4.72 million”.
Actually
1,940,000 + 2,610,000 = KWD 4,550,000. The report overstates income by KWD 170,000.
Land this
Claude will almost always repeat the stated total, because it reads the total as a fact rather than as a sum. Any number that is a total of other numbers has to be re-added by a person. This is the single most common way a wrong figure reaches a board pack.
F-02Internal contradiction §1 Executive summary vs §3 table

Retail occupancy is both 78% and 75%

§1 says
“Occupancy across the retail asset held at 78% for the quarter.”
§3 says
75.0% — and 36 let out of 48 units confirms 75%. The executive summary is the wrong one.
Land this
Ask which number Claude used in its summary. It usually takes the executive summary, because that text is nearer the top and reads more authoritative. The detail table is the source of truth; the summary is someone’s interpretation of it.
F-03Missing owner §10 Action log, row A-07

An action nobody owns, already overdue

Report says
A-07 “Reallocate unspent marketing budget” — Owner TBC, Due 30 Jun 2026, Status Open.
Actually
The report is dated 15 July 2026. The action was already two weeks overdue when the report was written, and no one is accountable for it. It is also the KWD 47,000 in §4 and a recommendation in §11 — three mentions, no owner.
Land this
Ask Claude “who owns each action?” and watch it either skip A-07 or quietly assign it to the Facilities Lead. Inventing a plausible owner is more dangerous than leaving a blank, because a blank gets noticed.
F-04Phantom decision §1 Executive summary vs §5 Construction

A decision that was never actually made

§1 says
“The Board approved the revised handover date at its June sitting. The tenant has been kept informed.”
§5 says
The date “has been proposed to the Board… It has not been ratified by the Board, and it has not been confirmed to the tenant in writing.”
Land this
This is the one that matters. A summary built from §1 will state a Board approval that does not exist — and that summary goes into a board pack, where someone acts on it. Separating what was decided from what was proposed is the reason the L1.5 prompt asks for those as two different lists. Most trainees miss this one; make sure the room sees it before the day ends.
F-05Impossible timeline §5 Construction

The handover date cannot happen

Report says
Joinery delivered 2 June 2026. Contractor requires six weeks from delivery to practical completion. Revised handover: 10 July 2026.
Actually
2 June + six weeks = 14 July 2026 at the earliest. The stated handover is four days before the work can finish. Either the date is wrong or the six-week duration is.
Land this
All three facts sit in the same small table, and Claude still will not flag the conflict — it reports each row faithfully without doing the arithmetic across them. Dates are just numbers; they need the same check as money.

Running the debrief

Roughly four minutes, at the end of Section 5.
1Ask before you tell

“What did you find?”

→
Collect what the room found first. Most tables land F-01 or F-03. Write them up.
→
Then reveal F-04. Ask what would have happened if that summary had gone to the Board unchecked. That question is the close of the day.
The takeaway to name
AI reads the document. You are still the one who is accountable for what it says.
