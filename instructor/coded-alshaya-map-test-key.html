MAP test — answer key

Thirty scored questions, thirty minutes, sat twice: on Day 1 before teaching begins and again on Day 3 before the capstone. The same paper both times. The gap between the two scores is the measure of what the workshop added.

Do not hand this out — and do not review answers on Day 1

Trainees sit the identical paper twice. If they leave Day 1 knowing which answers were right, the Day 3 score measures their memory of the debrief rather than their learning. Say only that the paper returns on Day 3, and move straight into the first session.

Paced for careful reading by an ESL cohort. Roughly 90 seconds of slack against the half hour.

Scoring and what the numbers mean

Score each sitting out of 30, then work with the difference rather than the totals.
Expected Day 1
9 – 15

Roughly a third to a half. Pure guessing scores about 8. A Day 1 score above 21 means the trainee arrived experienced — note it, and give them the harder scenario on Day 3.

Target Day 3
24 – 28

Eighty per cent or better. A Day 3 score below 19 needs a quiet conversation before the capstone teams are drawn.

Healthy gain
+10 or more

The headline figure for the client report. Report the cohort mean gain, not the mean score — it is the gain that the workshop is accountable for.

Watch items
3 · 23 · 24 · 25

Confidential data, connector approval and number verification. These carry real risk back to the desk, so treat anything under 90% correct on Day 3 as a reteach, whatever the total says.

Paired items — read these together
Four pairs test the same idea twice, once as a statement and once as an application: 2 and 27 (rewrite, don't restart), 3 and 23 (confidential data), 5 and 7 (what AI can and cannot do with data), and 10 and 18 (the prompt library). A trainee who gets the statement right and the application wrong has the rule but not the habit — that is coaching, not reteaching.
Reading the item analysis
Compare each question across the two sittings, not just the totals. A question that stays wrong for most of the room is a gap in the teaching, not in the trainees — note it for the next delivery. A question that most of the room already answered correctly on Day 1 is teaching time you can reinvest.
Pairing the two sittings
Responses are named, and the survey asks which sitting the trainee is taking. Match on the name field to build the pre/post pair for each person. The confidence question is not scored — it is there because confidence and score sometimes move apart, and a trainee whose confidence rose faster than their score is the one most likely to send an unverified number to a director.

Part 1 — True or False

Questions 1–10 · one mark each · about four minutes.
Q1Answer: TRUEDay 1 · §01 What is an LLM?
An AI language model writes its answer by predicting one word at a time, based on patterns in language.
The core mental model of the whole workshop. The model predicts the pattern; it is not looking anything up. Trainees who hold this understand why a model can be fluent and wrong at the same time, so every later lesson about verification follows from it. Pre-course, most people answer FALSE because they assume AI searches a database.
Q2Answer: FALSEDay 1 · §04 Refine, Don't Restart
When an AI draft is nearly right, the best step is to open a new chat and write the prompt again from the start.
Tests iterative prompting. The correct move is a small correction in the same conversation — "make it shorter", "make it warmer". Restarting throws away the context that was already working. This is the single habit that most changes how fast a beginner works.
Q3Answer: FALSEDay 1 · §01 What Can You Safely Type Into AI?
Tenant records and contract terms are safe to paste into a public AI tool if you delete the chat afterwards.
The data-handling rule, with the most common excuse attached to it. Deleting the chat afterwards does not undo the disclosure. Watch this item closely — it is one of the three where a wrong answer carries real risk back to the desk.
Q4Answer: TRUE NewDay 1 · §03 One Task, Two Ways to Ask
Giving an AI tool a sample of the style you want, before you make your request, is called few-shot prompting.
Vocabulary recall, and close to unguessable before the course — which makes it a clean pre/post discriminator. Trainees who know the term also know when to reach for it: when tone and format matter more than usual.
Q5Answer: FALSEDay 2 · §03 What AI can and cannot do with data
An AI tool can judge whether a financial result is good or bad for your company.
Separates calculation from interpretation. The model can compute the variance; it cannot know that 8% over budget is routine in one department and an emergency in another. That judgment stays with the trainee, and it is what their job actually is.
Q6Answer: TRUEDay 2 · §02 Features worth knowing — Export
A file exported from an AI presentation tool into PowerPoint can arrive with different spacing and fonts.
A small practical fact with a large consequence: the export is a draft, not a finished deck. Trainees who miss this present a broken deck to a director once, and only once.
Q7Answer: TRUE NewDay 2 · §03 What AI can and cannot do with data
An AI tool can find patterns and unusual values in a spreadsheet.
The positive half of the same split as question 5, and a useful counterweight to it. Some trainees arrive believing AI only handles words, and answer FALSE. A cohort that gets 5 right but 7 wrong has over-learned the caution and will under-use the tool — worth naming in the debrief.
Q8Answer: FALSE NewDay 1 · §04 Summarize the key points
Asking an AI tool to "summarise this" gives you a more useful result than describing the shape of summary you need.
"Summarise this" returns a paragraph, and you still do not know what you owe anyone. Naming the shape — decisions, open questions, owners — is what turns a summary into something you can act on. This is Format from CTFT, applied to summarising.
Q9Answer: TRUEDay 1 · §01 Four names you'll hear
Prompting skill learned in one AI tool carries over to other AI tools.
"The prompt is the skill, not the logo." This matters commercially: it is the answer to "what happens when our company standardises on a different tool?" Some trainees answer FALSE pre-course because they assume each tool must be learned separately.
Q10Answer: FALSE NewDay 2 · §05 Why save your best prompts?
A prompt library is a store of the best answers that an AI tool has given you.
A library holds the prompts, not the answers — an answer is worth reading once, a prompt is worth running every month. This is a genuine and common misconception, and it is the difference between a folder of old chats and a working asset.

Part 2 — Multiple choice

Questions 11–21 · one mark each · about ten minutes.
Q11Answer: BDay 1 · §03 The CTFT Framework
In the prompting framework taught in this course, what do the letters CTFT stand for?
A
Content, Target, Function, Timing
B
Context, Task, Format, Tone
C
Clarity, Topic, Feedback, Test
D
Context, Topic, Focus, Timing
Pure recall of the spine of the workshop. Expect near-zero pre-course and near-100% post-course — this item is the clearest single signal that Day 1 landed. All four options share the same shape, and two start with "Context", so it cannot be reached by elimination.
Q12Answer: DDay 1 · §01 What makes an LLM large?
In the term "large language model", what does the word "large" describe?
A
The number of human languages that the model can read and write
B
The maximum size of the file that you are allowed to upload
C
The greatest length of answer that the model can produce
D
The volume of text it learned from and the number of settings it holds
"Large is about scale, not intelligence." Option A is the intuitive wrong answer and usually leads pre-course. If A still leads post-course, the foundations session needs more time on the next run.
Q13Answer: C NewDay 1 · §01 What AI Does Well vs. Where It Fails
What is the name for an AI tool stating a fact that is wrong while sounding confident?
A
Bias
B
Drift
C
Hallucination
D
Overfitting
Names the concept that justifies every verification rule on the paper. All three distractors are real terms from the field, so a trainee cannot dismiss them as nonsense — but only one describes confident invention. Short options, so this is a fast item despite being high-value.
Q14Answer: ADay 1 · §01 What AI Does Well vs. Where It Fails
Which of these tasks is an AI tool strongest at?
A
Writing a first draft of a report
B
Confirming a figure held in your company's private records
C
Making the final decision on a contract renewal
D
Correcting data that was already wrong before it reached you
Every wrong option is taken directly from the taught "weak at" list — private context, final judgment, and bad source data. A trainee who has the strong/weak split straight answers this quickly.
Q15Answer: CDay 1 · §03 F = Format
Which part of a prompt states how long the answer should be and how it should be arranged?
A
Context
B
Tone
C
Format
D
Task
Checks that CTFT is understood as four distinct jobs, not memorised as a word. The common confusion is Format with Task, so watch for option D.
Q16Answer: A NewDay 1 · §05 Before You Send Any AI Draft
Before you send a document that an AI tool helped you write, which four things should you check?
A
Accuracy, tone, structure, and every number
B
Spelling, grammar, font size, and page count
C
Length, title, author, and date of writing
D
File format, file size, file name, and folder
The safety net for everything else on the paper. The distractors are all real checks a careful person might make — they are simply the cosmetic ones, and none of them would catch a wrong figure. That contrast is the teaching point.
Q17Answer: BDay 1 · §04 Connect it straight to your inbox
What does an MCP (Model Context Protocol) connector allow an AI assistant to do?
A
Run on your computer without an internet connection
B
Work inside tools you already use, such as mail, calendar and files
C
Check that every fact in a draft is correct
D
Store your prompts so that other staff can reuse them
The hardest recall item on the paper, and the most forward-looking — it is the difference between copy-and-paste and the tool working in place. Pair a low score here with the reminder in question 24.
Q18Answer: DDay 2 · §05 What makes a saved prompt worth keeping
What makes a saved prompt worth keeping in a prompt library?
A
It is short enough to remember without writing it down
B
It was written by a senior person in your department
C
It produced a result that needed no editing at all
D
It has a clear title and placeholders where the details change
Tests whether the prompt library is understood as reusable infrastructure rather than a folder of past chats. The placeholder idea is what makes a prompt survive contact with next month's data. Read alongside question 10.
Q19Answer: B NewDay 2 · §01 What goes into a presentation prompt
Which four things should a prompt for a presentation include?
A
The theme, the colour scheme, the number of images, and the font style
B
The type and length, the key points, the audience and purpose, and the tone
C
The title, the author, the date, and the department that owns it
D
The budget, the deadline, the approver, and the distribution list
"That is CTFT again, wearing a different hat." Option A is the trap, because it lists the things people think a design prompt needs. Naming look-and-feel before content is exactly how trainees produce a pretty deck that says nothing.
Q20Answer: ADay 2 · §01 The hybrid approach
Which way of using AI to build a presentation was recommended in this course?
A
Let AI build the outline and first draft, then add your own numbers and judgment
B
Let AI build the finished deck, then present it without any changes
C
Build the deck yourself, then ask AI to improve the design at the end
D
Build half of the slides yourself and let AI build the other half
"AI drafts. You own the version that gets presented." Option B is the failure mode the workshop exists to prevent; option D is a plausible-sounding split that misses the point, because the division of labour is by stage, not by slide count.
Q21Answer: CDay 2 · §02 Which one, when?
When is PowerPoint still a better choice than an AI presentation tool?
A
When you need a first draft of the deck quickly
B
When you want to change the style of every slide at once
C
When you must follow a company template and place each element exactly
D
When you want to send the deck to a colleague as a PDF file
Checks honest tool selection. Options A, B and D are each a job an AI tool does better, so the trainee has to know all four capabilities to answer. A correct answer here signals judgment rather than enthusiasm.

Part 3 — Work scenarios

Questions 22–30 · one mark each · about fourteen minutes.
Q22Answer: CDay 1 · §03 Weak Prompt vs. Strong Prompt
You manage leasing. You ask an AI tool: "Write something about the new parking policy." The answer is general and does not fit your building. What is the main reason?
A
The policy is too recent for the AI tool to know about it
B
The request should have been sent as a document, not as a chat message
C
The prompt gave no background, no structure and no tone
D
The AI tool you used was the wrong one for this kind of task
Diagnosis, not recall: the trainee must look at a bad result and locate the cause in their own prompt. Options A and D both blame the tool, which is exactly the pre-course instinct the workshop sets out to correct. One of the highest-value scenarios on the paper.
Q23Answer: ADay 1 · §01 What Can You Safely Type Into AI?
Your manager asks you for a summary of a confidential supplier contract. What should you do first?
A
Ask your manager or IT which tools are approved before you upload anything
B
Remove the supplier name, then paste the rest into a public AI tool
C
Paste the contract into a public AI tool and delete the chat afterwards
D
Summarise only the first ten pages, so that less of the contract is exposed
Options B and D are the dangerous ones, because both feel careful. Removing a name does not de-confidentialise commercial terms, and exposing a quarter of a contract is still exposing a contract. Partial caution is the most common real-world failure, so name it in the debrief.
Q24Answer: B NewDay 1 · §04 Connect it straight to your inbox
A colleague suggests connecting an AI assistant directly to the company mail system, so that it can draft replies in place. What should happen first?
A
Connect it for one department first, then review how it performs
B
Get approval from IT and your manager before anything is connected
C
Connect it with permission to read messages but not to send them
D
Ask the software provider to confirm that the connection is secure
"Nobody connects anything until IT and your manager have approved it." Every distractor is a sensible-sounding technical safeguard, and all three still start by connecting the tool to real company mail. The point is that the first step is a permission, not a precaution — this is the item most likely to prevent a real incident after the workshop.
Q25Answer: DDay 2 · §03 Three rules for analysing data
An AI tool reports that maintenance spending is 18 percent over budget. You want to put that figure in a report for the director. What should you do before you send the report?
A
Ask the AI tool to repeat the calculation in a new chat
B
Ask the AI tool to show the same figure as a chart
C
Round the figure down, so that the report stays careful
D
Recalculate the figure yourself from the source sheet
Option A is the trap: asking the same model again is not verification, it is a second opinion from the same source. Verification means leaving the tool and going back to the sheet. This item is the direct rehearsal for the capstone rule of one verified number per chart.
Q26Answer: A NewDay 2 · §03 Ask it to show its working
You upload a maintenance log and ask one question. The AI tool gives you a count of open tickets that looks too low. What is the most useful next step?
A
Ask it to list every figure it used and where each one came from
B
Ask the same question again, worded differently
C
Accept the number, because the tool read the whole sheet
D
Ask it to show the same count as a chart
The companion to question 25: that one asks what to do when a number matters, this one asks what to do when a number looks wrong. Making the tool show its sources usually exposes the real fault — a filter it applied, or rows it never read. Option D is the seductive one, because a chart feels like evidence while adding none.
Q27Answer: BDay 1 · §04 Rewrite: tone and length
You wrote a tenant notice with AI. The facts are correct, but it sounds cold and it is twice as long as you need. What is the most efficient next step?
A
Write a new prompt in a new chat that includes the tone and the length
B
In the same conversation, ask for a warmer tone and half the length
C
Edit the whole notice by hand until it is right
D
Generate the notice again in a different AI tool
Iterative prompting again, but applied rather than stated — pair the result with question 2. A trainee who answers 2 correctly and 27 wrongly knows the rule but has not yet made it a habit, which is a coaching problem, not a knowledge gap.
Q28Answer: ADay 2 · §01 Weak prompt vs. strong prompt
You need slides about a new maintenance request system. Which prompt is most likely to give you a usable first draft?
A
"Create a 5-slide presentation for property managers about the new maintenance request system. Cover the problem, the new process, staff responsibilities and the launch date. Professional tone."
B
"Create a professional presentation for our property managers that explains the maintenance request system and the reasons we are changing it."
C
"Create a short, well-designed set of slides on maintenance requests for the management team, covering everything that they need to know."
D
"Make a clear presentation about the new maintenance request system for the managers in our property team."
All four options name an audience and sound professional, so politeness cannot decide it. Only option A fixes the slide count and lists the ground to cover — it is the only one with Format. Distractor C is deliberately the vaguest while using the most confident words ("well-designed", "everything they need").
Q29Answer: D NewDay 2 · §05 HR: Screening Support
You are screening twelve candidates for one role. Which use of an AI tool fits the method taught in this course?
A
Ask it to rank the candidates and invite the top three to interview
B
Ask it to reject any candidate who does not meet every criterion
C
Ask it to choose the strongest candidate and draft the offer letter
D
Ask it to summarise each candidate against your criteria, then decide yourself
"The hiring decision never leaves the hiring manager." Each distractor hands over a slightly larger piece of that decision — shortlisting, rejecting, choosing — and A is the one most trainees will defend as reasonable. Summarising against criteria is support; ranking and inviting is deciding.
Q30Answer: CDay 2 · §03 Three rules · §04 The AI + Excel hybrid
Your team must present findings to the operations director tomorrow. You have a spreadsheet of 200 rows and two hours. Which order of work fits the method taught in this course?
A
Build the slides first, then ask AI to check whether the numbers are right
B
Ask AI every question in one long prompt, then copy the answer into the slides
C
Ask AI one clear question at a time, verify the key numbers by hand, then build the slides
D
Clean the whole sheet by hand first, then let AI build the slides and use its figures
The synthesis item, and the closest thing on the paper to the capstone itself. It carries all three rules at once: one question at a time, verify by hand, and analysis before presentation. Option D is the seductive wrong answer, because the hand-cleaning at the front makes it feel rigorous while the figures still go unverified at the end.
