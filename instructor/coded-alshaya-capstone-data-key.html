The seven planted inconsistencies

Each capstone scenario workbook (alshaya-capstone-s1.xlsx … s7.xlsx) carries exactly one deliberate inconsistency — between its sheets, or against its own totals. Every fault is catchable inside the team’s own file. The workbook README tells teams a fault exists; this page tells you what it is.

Do not hand this out

Teams know their workbook contains one planted inconsistency — that is printed on every workbook’s README — but not what it is. Confirm catches during the builds; reveal any misses in the Q&A after each team presents.

The inconsistencies in full

A-S1Cross-sheet alshaya-capstone-s1.xlsx · SLA & Attendance vs Maintenance Log

Contractor B’s June closures are inflated

Workbook says
The June summary on the SLA & Attendance sheet credits [CONTRACTOR B] with 6 tickets closed in June.
Actually
The workbook’s own Maintenance Log shows B closed exactly 1 ticket in June — the last on 3 June — while holding 15 open tickets. (D and In-house also closed 1 each; those figures are honest.)
Land this
This is the contractor’s own claimed number, and it flatters them. The log in the same file is the source of truth. Teams that catch it get their briefing’s sharpest line: “the contractor reports six June closures; the log shows one.”
A-S2Arithmetic alshaya-capstone-s2.xlsx · Cost Detail (Apr–Jun rows) vs Budget vs Actual

The maintenance months do not add up

Workbook says
Property Maintenance actuals for Apr, May and Jun: 14,800 + 16,200 + 21,900 = KWD 52,900.
Actually
The Budget vs Actual sheet in the same file says KWD 52,300 for the quarter. The monthly detail overstates by KWD 600 — and every other category reconciles exactly.
Land this
Same lesson as F-01, one level down: a detail sheet that disagrees with its own total. Every other category reconciles exactly, so a team that checks one column of sums finds it.
A-S3Counting alshaya-capstone-s3.xlsx · Unit Vacancy Schedule vs its summary

One unit too many is marked vacant

Workbook says
The unit rows show 35 let / 13 vacant. Unit F1-09 is marked Vacant.
Actually
The summary line on the same sheet — and the Occupancy & Income sheet in the same file — say 36 let / 12 vacant (75.0%). F1-09 is let on the rent roll; the schedule was not updated.
Land this
Ask Claude to count vacant units from the rows and it will say 13, contradicting the 75% everywhere else. The right move is to flag the conflict, not silently pick a side.
A-S4Transcription alshaya-capstone-s4.xlsx · Agency Long-List vs Candidate Register

C-03’s experience shrank in transcription

Workbook says
The Agency Long-List gives candidate C-03 (Property Manager) 8 years of experience.
Actually
The Candidate Register — the system of record, in the same file — says 12 years. The agency’s transcription is wrong.
Land this
An 8-vs-12 swing moves C-03 across the “6+ years” screen line on nobody’s decision but a typo’s. The lesson: screening data gets verified against the source system before anyone is ruled in or out.
A-S5Phantom record alshaya-capstone-s5.xlsx · Survey Comments vs Employee Survey

A thirteenth respondent who does not exist

Workbook says
The comments sheet has 13 rows — including E13 · Leasing: “Everything is excellent, no changes needed at all.”
Actually
The Employee Survey sheet in the same file has 12 responses, E01–E12. E13 exists nowhere — and it is the single most positive comment in the set.
Land this
A phantom record that flatters the result is exactly how survey data gets quietly massaged. Teams quoting “13 responses” or the E13 comment have not counted. Small samples get counted by hand.
A-S6Broken reference alshaya-capstone-s6.xlsx · Complaint Log vs Maintenance Log

A complaint linked to a ticket that does not exist

Workbook says
Complaint CMP-056 (AC / HVAC) links to ticket MT-097.
Actually
The Maintenance Log in the same file runs MT-001–MT-068. MT-097 does not exist. Every other linked ticket resolves.
Land this
A comms programme built on “every complaint gets a tracked ticket” dies on its first broken reference. Ask Claude to verify every linked ticket against the log — it is one prompt, and only one comes back missing.
A-S7Cross-sheet alshaya-capstone-s7.xlsx · Milestone Schedule vs Project Facts

The joinery delivery date is a week early

Workbook says
The Milestone Schedule records joinery delivered — actual 26 May 2026 — and forecasts practical completion 7 July and handover 14 July from it.
Actually
The Project Facts sheet in the same file (the confirmed position, from report §5) says joinery was delivered 2 June 2026. From 2 June, six weeks runs to 14 July at the earliest — practical completion, not handover.
Land this
The schedule is internally consistent and entirely wrong, because rev 4 was never updated after the slip. It also explains the report’s impossible 10 July handover (F-05): someone planned from the stale schedule. Dates get verified at the source, like money.
The takeaway to name
Same lesson as Day 1, now under deadline pressure: the model reads documents faithfully — including the wrong ones. A team of two with a named verifier catches in minutes what a rushed team ships to a director.
