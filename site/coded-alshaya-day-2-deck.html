AI for Presentations

The presentation problem

Blank slides, no structure, no design skill, and no time. Most people spend two to three hours building one deck — and most of that goes on layout, not on thinking.
That time comes straight out of your real workday.
The landscape

Four ways to let AI build the deck

We will use the first two today, so you can feel the difference between them.

Claude Design
Anthropic
Describe the deck inside the chat you are already working in. You get an editable visual canvas — click any element, change it, export it. Your context is already there.
We use this
Gamma
Gamma.app
A tool built only for presentations. Type a prompt, pick a theme, get a designed deck — then export it to PowerPoint or PDF.
We use this
Beautiful.ai
Beautiful.ai
Template-led. The layout adjusts itself as you add content, so slides stay tidy without you nudging boxes around.
Canva AI
Canva
Content and design in one place, with a very large library of images and brand assets behind it.
Different tools, one skill. The prompt is what carries across.

The hybrid approach

Use AI to generate the outline and the first draft. Then add your own expertise, your own numbers, and your own judgment before anyone else sees it.
AI drafts. You own the version that gets presented.

Framework

What goes into a presentation prompt

Type and length — “a 5-slide presentation”
Topic and key points — the specific ground it must cover
Audience and purpose — who is in the room, and what you need from them
Tone — professional, persuasive, plain
Compare

Weak prompt vs. strong prompt

Weak
“Make a presentation about maintenance.”
Strong
“Create a 5-slide presentation for property managers about the new maintenance request system. Cover the problem, the new process, staff responsibilities, and the launch date. Use a professional tone.”
That is CTFT again, wearing a different hat. Context, task, format, tone.

Choosing

Which one, when?

🎨
Claude Design
You are already deep in the content with Claude, and you want to shape the deck visually without leaving the conversation.
⚡
Gamma
You want a themed, presentable deck fast — and you need it out as PowerPoint or PDF at the end.
📊
PowerPoint
Company template, exact control over every element, or highly technical detail that must sit exactly where you put it.
The honest rule: AI for the first draft, your own tool for the final one — whenever the deck really matters.

Features worth knowing

📥
Import
Paste in an existing outline or document and let it structure the slides for you.
✏️
AI rewrite
Select one slide and ask for shorter, simpler, or more detail — without touching the rest.
🎭
Themes
Restyle the whole deck with one click when the first look is not right.
📤
Export
Out to PowerPoint or PDF. Always check the export — spacing and fonts can shift.

Same rule as Day 1. A new tool does not change it.

03
Data Analysis With AI

The data analysis problem

A spreadsheet with 200 rows lands in your inbox. No time to clean it, chart it, or work out what it means before the meeting starts.
Most people spend more time preparing data than analysing it.
Compare

What AI can and cannot do with data

AI cannot
Know whether a result is good or bad for your business, understand your organisation's context, or fix data that was wrong before it arrived
AI can
Calculate statistics, clean and sort, spot patterns and outliers, build charts, and draft the explanation
It will also do arithmetic confidently and occasionally get it wrong. Spot-check every number that matters.
Framework

Three rules for analysing data with Claude

One clear question at a time — not five at once
Spot-check every calculation — by hand, on the number that matters most
Ask it to show its working — “list every figure you used and where it came from”

04
Budget Analysis & the AI + Excel Hybrid

Why hybrid beats either tool alone

Claude explains what a pattern means and writes it up in plain language. Excel proves the number is actually correct. Use both, in that order.
Trust, but verify. Every time, no exceptions.

Prompt Libraries & Your Department

Why save your best prompts?

A prompt that worked once will work again. Save it, and next time you skip straight past the rewriting to the result.
This is your prompt library — you started it on Day 1 without noticing.
Framework

What makes a saved prompt worth keeping

A title you will recognise later — “monthly maintenance update”, not “prompt 3”
The full CTFT prompt, with placeholders where the details change
A note on which tool it worked best in — and what you had to fix afterwards

Where this lands in your job

👥
HR
Onboarding letters, policy summaries, job description drafts, training announcements.
💰
Finance
Variance commentary, budget summaries, turning a table into a paragraph a board can read.
🔧
Operations
Maintenance updates, contractor briefs, schedules, incident write-ups.
📣
Communications
Tenant notices, announcements, FAQs, and the same message rewritten for three audiences.
Every one of these is draft, rewrite, or summarize — the three moves from yesterday.
